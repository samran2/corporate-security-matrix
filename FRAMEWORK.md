# Corporate Security Matrix Operating Framework

Version: 1.1.0

Status: release candidate, non-normative management aid
Review date: 2026-08-29
Review cycle: annually or after material organizational, threat, regulatory or operating-model change

## Purpose

The operating framework turns the Corporate Security Matrix into a repeatable management practice. It helps an organization assign ownership, assess current capability, strengthen evidence, challenge confidence and prioritize improvement across nine security domains and five lifecycle phases.

It does not certify compliance with any cited standard. The cited sources inform domains as a whole; they do not prove every matrix entry or determine legal applicability. Organizations should tailor the framework to their size, sector, jurisdiction, contracts, risk profile and professional obligations.

## Principles

1. **One accountable owner.** Every material domain and lifecycle gap has a named decision owner, delivery lead and escalation path.
2. **Evidence over intention.** Assess what can be demonstrated today, not what policy says should happen.
3. **Risk-shaped depth.** Apply deeper assurance where criticality, exposure, duties or credible harm justify it.
4. **Cross-functional decisions.** Review physical, people, cyber, operational, legal, safety and continuity dependencies together.
5. **Test the system.** Use exercises, incidents, inspections and control testing to reveal weak coordination and false confidence.
6. **Improve continuously.** Convert events, near misses and findings into owned corrective action and verified learning.

## Maturity model

The 0–4 maturity scale is a project-defined, non-normative heuristic. It is not taken from a cited standard, is not a certification scale and must not be presented as a conformity rating.

Score each lifecycle phase independently from 0 to 4:

| Level | Name | Observable state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Exposed | Material exposure is not consistently known, owned or addressed. |
| 1 | Reactive | Response depends on individuals and is activated mainly after events. |
| 2 | Defined | Minimum controls, ownership and escalation paths are documented. |
| 3 | Assured | Controls operate consistently and are tested with credible evidence. |
| 4 | Adaptive | Signals, learning and changing conditions continuously shape decisions. |

Do not average away a critical blind spot. Report the maturity of each phase and explicitly identify the weakest material phase.

## Assessment confidence

Report capability and confidence separately. The following 50/30/20 weighting is an optional, project-defined heuristic rather than an external benchmark:

- 50% control evidence: current records, configurations, logs, inspections or observed practice;
- 30% independent challenge: review by a competent party outside the delivery owner's direct line; and
- 20% exercise recency: evidence from a recent test, incident, exercise or disruption.

Score each component from 0 to 4 and normalize the weighted result to 0–100. Keep the three component scores visible so the weighted total cannot hide weak evidence. Record assumptions and unavailable evidence beside the score, and do not compare scores between organizations without a shared calibration method.

## Governance

| Role | Core accountability |
| --- | --- |
| Board or oversight body | Set risk appetite, challenge systemic exposure and require evidence of readiness. |
| Executive sponsor | Own cross-enterprise outcomes, resolve trade-offs and accept or escalate residual risk. |
| Framework lead | Maintain taxonomy, assessment quality, review cadence and cross-domain coherence. |
| Domain owner | Deliver capability, maintain evidence and close corrective actions. |
| Independent assurance | Challenge material claims without delivery conflicts and verify closure. |
| Workforce and partners | Apply controls, report weak signals and participate in testing and learning. |

Small organizations may combine roles, but decision rights, conflicts and escalation routes should remain explicit.

## Operating cadence

The weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual intervals below are illustrative starting points, not requirements from a cited standard. Adapt them to risk, regulatory duties, incident conditions and decision latency.

- **Weekly signal review:** material incidents, near misses, threat changes, control failures and overdue emergency actions.
- **Monthly domain review:** exposure, evidence, dependencies, corrective actions and decisions requiring cross-functional support.
- **Quarterly executive review:** systemic gaps, resource trade-offs, residual risk and readiness for priority scenarios.
- **Annual re-baseline:** context, materiality, ownership, sources, maturity targets and assurance plan.

Increase the cadence for high-hazard operations, major transformation, elevated threat conditions or active remediation.

## Adoption path

The 30/90/180/365-day path is an illustrative implementation sequence, not a universal timetable or a standard requirement.

- **30 days — Establish:** name the sponsor and owners, select priority domains, and agree materiality and escalation routes.
- **90 days — Baseline:** assess priority phases, record evidence and uncertainty, and fund urgent gap reduction.
- **180 days — Exercise:** test cross-domain scenarios and validate decision rights, communications, dependencies and recovery assumptions.
- **365 days — Assure:** complete independent challenge, refresh targets and publish an evidence-backed improvement roadmap.

## Illustrative implementation aids

The integrated release-candidate working tree associates each of the 180 canonical Controls & actions entries with two short implementation aids. An aid may describe a register, checklist, workflow, platform category or other mechanism that can help put the mitigation into practice; it is not a control result or evidence that the mitigation operates effectively.

These aids are illustrative and vendor-neutral. They do not endorse a product, prescribe procurement, determine compliance or guarantee risk elimination. Select, configure, test and govern any aid for the organization's risk, workforce, technology, jurisdiction and professional-practice context. Automated screens can detect structural defects and a bounded set of vendor or absolute-claim patterns, but competent human review remains necessary for semantic fit, safety, privacy and legal applicability.

## Full governance record for material findings

The local 45-cell browser assessment is a screening summary, not this full governance record. It intentionally does not collect evidence documents, personal data or attachments. Keep the additional fields below in an approved organizational system outside this static site.

For each material matrix entry, record:

- domain and lifecycle phase;
- applicability and business context;
- accountable owner;
- current maturity level and target level;
- supporting evidence and evidence date;
- confidence score and reviewer;
- residual risk decision;
- corrective action, due date and verification status; and
- relevant laws, standards, contracts or internal policies.

## Limitations

The framework and its illustrative implementation aids are planning and management aids. They are not legal advice, certified life-safety engineering, a country-specific compliance determination, an emergency procedure, a certification method, a procurement recommendation or an assurance opinion. Laws, regulations, permits, employment rights, privacy duties, contractual obligations and professional requirements must be determined for the relevant jurisdiction, sector and operating context.
