# Source Library

Edition: 1.1.0
Reviewed: 2026-08-29
Status: release-candidate source set; current public release `v1.0.0`

The matrix is original, evidence-led synthesis. Its official and authoritative source records provide domain context, terminology and control logic.

These sources inform each domain and, where explicitly mapped, its phase context; they do not establish compliance or prove every individual matrix entry. A link labelled as an official record may lead to publication metadata rather than the licensed full text. Always consult the complete source and competent advice before relying on it for a high-consequence decision.

## Source selection and interpretation

- International standards, intergovernmental guidance and official public-sector publications are preferred.
- ISO links are official publication records. They do not reproduce the full standards or confer certification.
- ILO conventions and the United Nations Convention against Corruption have legal effect through ratification, national implementation and the duties of States Parties; they are not presented as identical direct corporate law in every country.
- NIST, CISA and OSHA material is published in the United States. NIST and CISA material is used for transferable guidance with its intended audience shown; OSHA regulations apply only where the relevant United States legal conditions are met.
- ENISA material provides European Union threat context, not a universal control baseline.
- IFC, IFRC, WHO and World Bank material must be applied within its stated audience and technical scope.
- EK is a Finland-specific supporting comparator, not the governing source base.
- Applicability, legal effect and implementation must be checked for the relevant organization, jurisdiction, sector, contract and operating context.

## Domain mappings

### People and Personnel Security

Context: trusted access, work travel, workplace violence and harassment, psychosocial health, and critical roles. Psychosocial distress is not evidence of malicious intent. Screening and monitoring must be role-based, risk-proportionate, privacy-preserving and lawful.

- ISO 31030:2021 Travel risk management — Guidance for organizations: <https://www.iso.org/standard/54204.html>
- CISA Personal Security Considerations Action Guide: <https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/personal-security-considerations-action-guide>
- CISA Resources for Onboarding and Employment Screening Fact Sheet: <https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/resources-onboarding-and-employment-screening-fact-sheet>
- CISA Insider Threat Mitigation Resources and Tools: <https://www.cisa.gov/topics/physical-security/insider-threat-mitigation/resources-and-tools>
- ILO Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190), NORMLEX record: <https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A12100%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP12100_ILO_CODE%3AC190>
- WHO Guidelines on Mental Health at Work: <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240053052>

### Physical and Site Security

Context: protective design, site planning, access control and physical resilience. Lawful protest is distinct from violence or access disruption. Counter-unmanned-aircraft actions must remain within local authority; the source pack does not authorize jamming or interdiction.

- CISA Hometown Security: <https://www.cisa.gov/hometown-security>
- CISA Be Air Aware: <https://www.cisa.gov/topics/physical-security/be-air-aware>
- IFC General Environmental, Health, and Safety Guidelines: <https://www.ifc.org/en/insights-reports/general-environmental-health-and-safety-guidelines>
- ISO 22341:2021 Security and resilience — Protective security — Guidelines for crime prevention through environmental design: <https://www.iso.org/standard/50078.html>
- ISO 22342:2023 Security and resilience — Protective security — Guidelines for the development of a security plan for an organization: <https://www.iso.org/standard/50079.html>

### Emergency Preparedness and Life Safety

Context: site-specific reasonably foreseeable hazards, emergency action, evacuation, first response and incident coordination. First aid must stay within competence; formal clinical triage belongs to trained and authorized responders. Life-safety systems require local-code compliance and competent professional design and maintenance.

- OSHA Emergency Preparedness and Response: <https://www.osha.gov/emergency-preparedness/getting-started>
- OSHA 29 CFR 1910.38 Emergency action plans: <https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.38>
- OSHA Fight or Flee? Fire Extinguisher Use Decisions: <https://www.osha.gov/etools/evacuation-plans-procedures/eap/fight-or-flee>
- IFC General Environmental, Health, and Safety Guidelines: <https://www.ifc.org/en/insights-reports/general-environmental-health-and-safety-guidelines>
- ISO 22320:2018 Security and resilience — Emergency management — Guidelines for incident management: <https://www.iso.org/standard/67851.html>
- IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025: <https://www.ifrc.org/document/ifrc-international-first-aid-resuscitation-and-education-guidelines-2025>
- WHO Mass Casualty Management in Emergency Units: <https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/clinical-services-and-systems/emergency-and-critical-care/mass-casualty-management>

### Operations, Supply Chain and Quality

Context: general risk, security management, supply continuity and quality systems. NIST C-SCRM applies specifically to ICT/OT product and service supply chains. Payment, customs, product-safety, withdrawal and recall duties must be validated by sector and jurisdiction.

- NIST Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management publications: <https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/cyber-supply-chain-risk-management/publications>
- ISO 28000:2022 Security and resilience — Security management systems — Requirements, including Amendment 1:2024: <https://www.iso.org/standard/79612.html>
- ISO 31000:2018 Risk management — Guidelines: <https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html>
- NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1 Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations: <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final>
- ISO 9001:2015 Quality management systems — Requirements, including Amendment 1:2024: <https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html>
- ISO/TS 22318:2021 Security and resilience — Business continuity management systems — Guidelines for supply chain continuity management: <https://www.iso.org/standard/79001.html>

### Environmental and Hazardous Materials Safety

Context: emissions, waste, releases and impacts to air, water, soil, ecosystems and communities. Worker exposure and occupational heat sit primarily in Occupational Health and Safety; evacuation, medical response and incident command sit in Emergency Preparedness. Hazardous-material prevention and containment are shared interfaces.

- ISO 14001:2026 Environmental management systems — Requirements with guidance for use: <https://www.iso.org/standard/14001>
- IFC General Environmental, Health, and Safety Guidelines: <https://www.ifc.org/en/insights-reports/general-environmental-health-and-safety-guidelines>
- ISO 50001:2018 Energy management systems — Requirements with guidance for use, including Amendment 1:2024: <https://www.iso.org/standard/69426.html>
- WHO Chemical Incidents: <https://www.who.int/health-topics/chemical-incidents/>
- WHO Chemical Safety: <https://www.who.int/health-topics/chemical-safety>

### Information and Cybersecurity

Context: cyber-risk governance, voluntary baselines, information-security management, incident response and European Union threat context. NIST CSF 2.0 has six functions, including Govern; the Matrix treats governance as cross-cutting while retaining five operational lifecycle phases. Legal, privacy and notification duties must be checked by jurisdiction and contract.

- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0: <https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20>
- CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals: <https://www.cisa.gov/cybersecurity-performance-goals>
- NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management: A CSF 2.0 Community Profile: <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final>
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Information security management systems — Requirements, including Amendment 1:2024: <https://www.iso.org/standard/27001>
- NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, with Release 5.2.0 status: <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final>
- ENISA Threat Landscape 2025, version 1.2: <https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/enisa-threat-landscape-2025>

### Integrity, Compliance and Investigations

Context: anti-corruption, broader compliance management, whistleblowing and internal investigations. Employment rights, privilege, privacy, evidence handling and reporting duties remain jurisdiction-specific. Politically exposed person status is not itself evidence of misconduct.

- United Nations Convention against Corruption, full text: <https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf>

### Occupational Health and Safety

Context: occupational-safety systems, worker participation, hierarchy of controls, health surveillance and safe work. Employers and line management retain accountability; workers and representatives participate in design, reporting and improvement. Health information requires confidentiality, justification and competent occupational-health oversight.

- ILO-OSH 2001 Guidelines: <https://www.ilo.org/resource/guidelines-occupational-safety-and-health-management-systems-ilo-osh-2001>
- ISO 45001:2018 Occupational health and safety management systems — Requirements with guidance for use, including Amendment 1:2024: <https://www.iso.org/standard/63787.html>
- IFC General Environmental, Health, and Safety Guidelines — Occupational Health and Safety: <https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2000/2007-general-ehs-guidelines-occupational-health-and-safety-en.pdf>
- ILO Occupational Safety and Health Convention, 1981 (No. 155), NORMLEX record: <https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A12100%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP12100_ILO_CODE%3AC155>
