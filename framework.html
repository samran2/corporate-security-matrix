Operating framework

From coverage to confidence.

A lightweight management model for assigning ownership, testing controls, strengthening evidence and making enterprise-security decisions on a repeatable cadence.

01 · Design principles

A framework leaders can actually operate.

Use the model as a decision aid. Tailor it to organizational context, applicable law, contractual obligations and the professional standards relevant to each domain.

01

One accountable owner

Every domain and material lifecycle gap has a named decision owner, delivery lead and escalation path.

02

Evidence over intention

Assess what can be demonstrated today—not what a policy says should happen or what a future project might deliver.

03

Risk-shaped depth

Apply deeper assurance where criticality, exposure, regulatory duties or credible harm justify it.

04

Cross-functional decisions

Bring security, operations, people, legal, technology, safety and continuity into the same operating picture.

05

Test the system

Use exercises, incidents, inspections and control tests to reveal weak coordination and false confidence.

06

Improve continuously

Turn every material event, near miss and review finding into owned corrective action and verified learning.

02 · Maturity

Five observable levels of capability.

Score each lifecycle phase independently. A domain is only as resilient as its weakest material phase, so do not average away a critical blind spot.

LEVEL 0

Exposed

Material exposure is not consistently known, owned or addressed.

LEVEL 1

Reactive

Response depends on individuals and is activated mainly after events.

LEVEL 2

Defined

Minimum controls, ownership and escalation paths are documented.

LEVEL 3

Assured

Controls operate consistently and are tested with credible evidence.

LEVEL 4

Adaptive

Signals, learning and changing conditions continuously shape decisions.

03 · Assessment

Separate capability from confidence.

04 · Governance

Clear decisions at every level.

Keep oversight distinct from delivery and independent challenge. Small organizations may combine roles, but decision rights and conflicts should remain explicit.

BOARD / OVERSIGHT

Set risk appetite

  • Approve material tolerance and investment principles.
  • Challenge systemic gaps and overdue exposure.
  • Require evidence of readiness for critical scenarios.

EXECUTIVE SPONSOR

Resolve trade-offs

  • Own cross-enterprise outcomes.
  • Remove delivery and funding barriers.
  • Accept or escalate material residual risk.

FRAMEWORK LEAD

Maintain coherence

  • Operate the review cadence.
  • Maintain taxonomy and assessment quality.
  • Connect risks across domain boundaries.

DOMAIN OWNER

Deliver capability

  • Maintain controls and evidence.
  • Own corrective actions.
  • Escalate changing exposure quickly.

INDEPENDENT ASSURANCE

Challenge confidence

  • Test material claims and samples.
  • Report gaps without delivery conflicts.
  • Track closure to verified outcomes.

WORKFORCE / PARTNERS

Surface reality

  • Report weak signals and near misses.
  • Apply controls in daily work.
  • Participate in exercises and learning.

05 · Operating cadence

Review at the speed of changing risk.

The cadence below is a baseline. Increase frequency for high-hazard operations, major transformation, elevated threat conditions or active remediation.

WEEKLY

Signal review

Triage material incidents, near misses, threat changes, control failures and overdue emergency actions.

MONTHLY

Domain review

Review exposure, evidence, dependencies, action progress and decisions that require cross-functional support.

QUARTERLY

Executive review

Challenge systemic gaps, resource trade-offs, residual risk and preparedness for priority scenarios.

ANNUALLY

Re-baseline

Refresh context, materiality, ownership, source mappings, maturity targets and the assurance plan.

06 · Adoption path

Start focused. Build evidence. Scale deliberately.

Begin with the scenarios and dependencies that could cause the greatest harm. Do not attempt to score every entry before ownership and assessment criteria are agreed.

30

Establish

Name the sponsor and owners. Select priority domains. Agree materiality, terms and escalation routes.

90

Baseline

Assess priority lifecycle phases. Record evidence and uncertainty. Fund urgent gap reduction.

180

Exercise

Test cross-domain scenarios. Validate decision rights, communications, dependencies and recovery assumptions.

365

Assure

Complete independent challenge, refresh targets and publish an evidence-backed improvement roadmap.

Apply the framework

Start with the domain that matters most now.

Use the matrix to frame the exposure, compare lifecycle coverage and identify the next decision that needs an owner.
